# Visits to Fernglen

Fernglen Native Plant Gardens has always been a place of education. This began way back in the 1900s with the notable T.F. Cheeseman of the Auckland Museum, Professor Lancaster of the Auckland University, H.B. Dobbie. Frank Fisher and Bill and  into the 2000s, Muriel Fisher, shared their extensive knowledge of rare and endangered plants.

Today this education continues with very knowledgeable members of the Fernglen Gardens Educational committee and trust, and Landscape Solutions, Fernglen Curator Deborah, who works in the gardens on Tuesdays and Fridays.   

## School/Educational Sessions Welcome

Education sessions can be hosted to your needs and level. All sessions can be related to the NZ Curriculum with opportunity for practical field work and or environmental emphasis.

- **Local History** - a journey through time on the Fisher's property, owned by the Fisher family since 1888 and now an Auckland Council Reserve.
- **Native plant identification** - observation, seed collecting and planting.
- **Birds and their food sources** - all you want to know about the tūī, kererū, kotare, riroriro, fantail, morepork, shining cuckoo, silver eye.
- **Minibeasts** - discover and observe habitats, behaviour and life cycles, invertebrate sampling, beneficial and harmful effects on plants.
- **Endangered Plants, Wetland Habitat, Nature Trails, Night Walks**

## Information

- Whole class visits and group bookings
- **Education centre** available catering for about 30 people - whiteboard, activities
- **Night walks** listening for night birds, spotting nautical creatures and glow worms

Self-guided tours - Fernglen is open to the public, feel free to guide yourselves with the help of the maps and self-guided tours at the kiosk inside the gate.
